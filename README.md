# My library

Interactive editions of public-domain books, with invented readers from different centuries asking questions in the margins.

## Books

- [Crime and Punishment](crime-and-punishment/index.html), Fyodor Dostoevsky (1866), translated by Constance Garnett (1914). The full text, with 415 opening questions from six readers across all 41 chapters.

## How the margins work

Each reader opens with a question about a phrase in the text. Answer it, and the reader replies with their own view. Readers know the novel only up to the chapter you are on, unless you switch that off at the foot of the page. Quotations in the margins are checked against the text.

The live readers are written by Claude and work when the page is opened as a claude.ai artifact. Opened anywhere else, the pre-written questions still show and you can write in the margins yourself, but the readers cannot answer.

## Sources

The text of *Crime and Punishment* is from Project Gutenberg ebook 2554 and is in the public domain.
